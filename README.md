# Hippocampus-Inspired Planning Under Model Bias

**A reproducible tabular reinforcement-learning study of when simulated planning helps—and when a biased learned model can hurt it.**

This repository began as grid-world notebooks exploring cognitive-map and hippocampal-replay-inspired ideas, then developed into a controlled study of **reliability-weighted Dyna-Q**. The current research focus is narrow: whether an agent can use its own past model-prediction accuracy to reduce the impact of deliberately corrupted transitions during planning.

> **Scope note:** “Hippocampus-inspired” describes a computational analogy between simulated experience in model-based RL and replay-related ideas in neuroscience. This project does not implement a biological hippocampus or establish a neural mechanism.

## Research question

Dyna-Q learns from real environment interactions and also performs extra temporal-difference (TD) updates using transitions generated by a learned model. These simulated updates can propagate value information quickly. However, if the planning model predicts the wrong next state, repeated updates can reinforce a poor policy.

This project asks:

> Can a planning agent reduce the damage from biased simulated transitions by scaling each planning update according to its previous prediction–observation agreement?

## Method: reliability-weighted Dyna-Q

For each state–action pair, the agent makes a model prediction before observing the real next state. After the environment transition, it compares the prediction with the observation and updates a pair-specific empirical reliability score. The score affects **later simulated planning updates only**; real-transition Q-learning updates retain weight 1.

For the deterministic reliability experiment, the smoothed score is:

\[
w(s,a)=\frac{m(s,a)+1}{n(s,a)+2}, \quad n(s,a)>0,
\]

where \(m(s,a)\) is the number of matched predictions and \(n(s,a)\) is the number of scored predictions. Before any prediction has been scored, the implementation uses weight zero.

The planning update is:

\[
Q(s,a)\leftarrow Q(s,a)+\alpha\,w(s,a)\,\hat{\delta}(s,a).
\]

Here \(\hat{\delta}\) is the TD error generated by a simulated transition. This reliability score is an **empirical trust heuristic, not a calibrated probability of model correctness or a general uncertainty estimator**.

The stochastic-transition extension uses a separate probability-aware predictive-skill score based on capped prequential negative log likelihood (NLL). Its estimator and results are documented separately because stochastic outcomes require a different scoring rule than exact next-state agreement.

## Main components

- **Grid-world environments:** small deterministic and stochastic maze tasks with fixed start and goal locations.
- **Model-free reference:** tabular Q-learning, which updates from real transitions only.
- **Planning references:** fixed-epsilon Dyna-Q, adaptive-epsilon Dyna-Q, unweighted planning, shuffled-reliability control, and prioritized sweeping in the relevant experiment suites.
- **Reliability-weighted planning:** scales simulated TD updates using prior prediction history.
- **Adaptive-exploration diagnostic:** tests a Q-value-dispersion-based epsilon heuristic. This is treated as a baseline, not as validated epistemic uncertainty.
- **Additional exploratory baselines:** count-based exploration and discrete-state RND-, ICM-, and NGU-style adaptations, plus an exploratory UCT-MCTS/Q-value-bootstrap hybrid.

## Selected results

The results below are from distinct experiment tracks. They use different environment sets and should **not** be pooled into one headline number.

### 1. Original deterministic factorial experiment

The main factorial experiment contains **1,800 algorithm-condition runs**: six deterministic 8×8 maze layouts (three development and three held-out), five planning-model corruption levels (0%, 5%, 10%, 20%, 30%), six tabular algorithms, and ten seeds per condition. Each trained policy is evaluated greedily over ten episodes.

Greedy evaluation success (%), averaged over 60 maze–seed units per corruption level:

| Planning-model corruption | Q-learning | Fixed Dyna-Q | Adaptive Dyna-Q | Reliability-weighted Dyna-Q | No weighting | Shuffled reliability |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0%  | 100.0 | 100.0 | 100.0 | 100.0 | 100.0 | 100.0 |
| 5%  | 100.0 | 36.7 | 13.3 | 100.0 | 46.7 | 60.0 |
| 10% | 100.0 | 18.3 | 5.0 | 100.0 | 20.0 | 26.7 |
| 20% | 100.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 100.0 | 3.3 | 1.7 |
| 30% | 100.0 | 1.7 | 0.0 | 100.0 | 1.7 | 0.0 |

**Interpretation:** in this specific deterministic benchmark, reliability weighting protected Dyna-style planning against the tested planning-only corruption and outperformed the planning controls. Q-learning also retained 100% success because it does not use the corrupted planning predictions. These results therefore **do not establish superiority over model-free Q-learning** or broad generalization to other environments.

At 30% corruption, mean steps during the final 40 training episodes were 18.35 ± 1.25 for reliability-weighted Dyna-Q, 119.45 ± 29.93 for fixed Dyna-Q, and 17.62 ± 0.54 for Q-learning. This is a training-efficiency result for the tested setup, not a wall-clock or compute-matched comparison.

### 2. Larger deterministic grids and prioritized sweeping

A separate **840-run** extension tested four fixed mazes (12×12 and 16×16, with shortest-path lengths of 22, 30, 25, and 40 moves), three corruption levels (0%, 10%, 30%), seven algorithms, and ten seeds.

Across the four maze layouts and seeds, evaluation success for reliability-weighted Dyna-Q was **100%, 90%, and 60%** at 0%, 10%, and 30% corruption, respectively. Fixed Dyna-Q fell to **2.5%** at 10% corruption and **0%** at 30%; prioritized sweeping also fell to **0%** at those two corruption levels in this run.

**Important caveat:** Q-learning achieved 0% greedy evaluation success in this 200-episode extension, despite a reported late-training success statistic that was much higher. That discrepancy remains insufficiently explained and should be treated as a diagnostic issue—not as evidence that Q-learning is intrinsically worse. The layouts are fixed and this experiment does not establish generalization over a distribution of mazes.

### 3. Stochastic transitions

A separate **840-run** experiment used two fixed maze layouts, action-slip probabilities of 10% and 25%, planning-model corruption levels of 0%, 10%, and 30%, seven algorithms, and ten seeds. The action-slip process remained active during training and greedy evaluation.

Selected greedy evaluation success rates (%):

| Action slip | Planning corruption | Q-learning | Fixed Dyna-Q | Reliability-weighted Dyna-Q | No-weight control | Prioritized sweeping |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10% | 0%  | 31.0 | 100.0 | 98.0 | 95.5 | 100.0 |
| 10% | 10% | 31.0 | 26.0  | 84.0 | 28.5 | 0.0 |
| 10% | 30% | 31.0 | 2.0   | 51.0 | 10.5 | 0.0 |
| 25% | 0%  | 36.5 | 100.0 | 72.0 | 99.5 | 95.0 |
| 25% | 10% | 36.5 | 27.5  | 82.5 | 28.5 | 1.5 |
| 25% | 30% | 36.5 | 12.5  | 51.0 | 16.0 | 0.0 |

**Interpretation:** reliability weighting improved performance relative to several planning controls when the planning model was corrupted. However, it was **not uniformly best**: at 25% action slip and zero injected corruption, reliability-weighted Dyna-Q reached 72% success while fixed Dyna-Q reached 100%. This exposes a limitation of the current reliability heuristic under stochastic transitions. Confidence intervals from these experiments are conditional on the two fixed layouts and the tested corruption masks; they are not evidence of generalization to arbitrary stochastic environments.

### 4. Intrinsic-exploration adaptations

A separate 200-run experiment compared tabular Q-learning with count-based exploration and discrete-state RND-, ICM-, and NGU-style adaptations on two stochastic mazes.

On the easier 12×12 maze, observed success rates at 10% / 25% action slip were:

| Method | 10% slip | 25% slip |
|---|---:|---:|
| Q-learning | 62% | 73% |
| Count-bonus Q-learning | 25% | 71% |
| RND-style Q-learning | 37% | 76% |
| ICM-style Q-learning | 8% | 0% |
| NGU-style Q-learning | 61% | 67% |

All tested methods recorded 0% success on the harder 16×16 maze under both slip settings within the 200-episode budget. These are **small discrete-state adaptations**, not canonical reproductions of the original deep-RL RND, ICM, or NGU methods. The results do not support a general ranking of those algorithms.

### 5. MCTS: explored but excluded from the main comparison

The repository includes an exploratory UCT tree-search implementation that combines an online empirical transition model, real-transition tabular Q-learning, and Q-value bootstrapping at unfinished search leaves. It is explicitly a **hybrid**, not a canonical MCTS-RL reproduction, and its computational budget is not matched to Dyna-Q's five one-step planning updates.

The target-maze runs failed to learn reliably: the final 120-run track had very low evaluation success on the 12×12 maze and 0% on the 16×16 maze; a subsequent 20-run near-zero-slip validation also recorded 0% evaluation success. A small open 8×8 diagnostic showed a more promising result for a return-first root-action rule, but it used only three seeds and 60 training episodes per seed. This pilot is not enough to validate MCTS on the study's target mazes.

For that reason, MCTS is **excluded from the main performance claims**. These failures indicate that the tested implementation/configuration was inadequate for this task and budget; they do not show that MCTS is generally inferior.

## Installation

Python 3.10+ is recommended for the experiment suites. From the repository root:

```bash
git clone https://github.com/JainHarsh00/HippocampusReasearch.git
cd HippocampusReasearch
python -m venv .venv
```

Activate the environment:

```powershell
# Windows PowerShell
.venv\Scripts\Activate.ps1
```

```bash
# macOS / Linux
source .venv/bin/activate
```

Install the root requirements:

```bash
python -m pip install --upgrade pip
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
```

The root requirements include NumPy and Matplotlib. Some experiment packages provide their own `requirements.txt`; install dependencies from the README in the package you intend to run.

## Reproducing experiments

The command blocks below are standalone examples. Start from the repository root for each package command (or return to the root before running a different block). Run a package smoke test first; the full runs can take substantial time.

### A. Run the initial baseline

From the repository root:

```bash
python run_experiments.py --seeds 10 --episodes 200 --max-steps 150 --planning-steps 5 --eval-episodes 10 --output results
```

This is an initial single-maze diagnostic comparing Q-learning, fixed Dyna-Q, count-curiosity Dyna-Q, adaptive Dyna-Q, and curiosity plus adaptive Dyna-Q. It is not the main multi-maze reliability-weighting experiment.

### B. Adaptive-epsilon diagnostic

From the repository root:

```bash
python diagnose_adaptive_epsilon.py --seeds 10 --episodes 200 --planning-steps 5 --lambdas 0,0.5,1,2,5,10
```

This tests sensitivity to the dispersion parameter. The adaptive-epsilon rule is a heuristic based on the range of current Q-values, not a validated epistemic-uncertainty estimate.

### C. Reliability-weighting experiment

```bash
cd reliability-awareDynaQ
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python run_reliability_aware.py --seeds 10 --episodes 200 --planning-steps 5 --eval-episodes 10 --bias-levels 0,0.05,0.10,0.20,0.30
```

This package's full default design covers three development mazes, five corruption levels, four algorithms, and ten seeds. The separately archived held-out validation experiment adds three further layouts. Read the package README before comparing runs, since configurations and scopes differ.

### D. Larger grids and prioritized sweeping

```bash
cd hippocampus_scale_prioritized_sweeping_extension
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python run_scale_ps_validation.py --seeds 10 --episodes 200 --max-steps 250 --planning-steps 5 --eval-episodes 10 --bias-levels 0,0.10,0.30 --bootstrap-resamples 30000 --output results_scale_ps
```

### E. Stochastic-transition validation

```bash
cd hippocampus_stochastic_transition_extension
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python run_stochastic_reliability.py --seeds 10 --episodes 200 --max-steps 250 --planning-steps 5 --eval-episodes 10 --slip-levels 0.10,0.25 --bias-levels 0,0.10,0.30 --bootstrap-resamples 30000 --output results_stochastic
```

### F. Additional intrinsic baselines and exploratory MCTS

From the repository root, enter the corrected suite:

```bash
cd additional_baselines_suite_corrected
```

Run the intrinsic-exploration smoke test first:

```bash
python run_intrinsic_baselines.py --seeds 1 --episodes 3 --max-steps 12 --eval-episodes 2 --slip-levels 0.10 --curiosity-train-every 2 --output smoke_intrinsic
```

Then the full intrinsic-exploration experiment:

```bash
python run_intrinsic_baselines.py --seeds 10 --episodes 200 --max-steps 250 --eval-episodes 10 --slip-levels 0.10,0.25 --intrinsic-scale 0.05 --curiosity-train-every 4 --output results_intrinsic
```

Run the MCTS smoke test only if you want to inspect the exploratory hybrid:

```bash
python run_mcts_baseline.py --seeds 1 --episodes 3 --max-steps 12 --eval-episodes 2 --slip-levels 0.10 --bias-levels 0,0.30 --mcts-simulations 4 --search-depth 24 --output smoke_mcts
```

The corresponding full MCTS command is documented in this package's README. MCTS did not learn reliably in the completed target-maze runs, so do not treat the smoke test as a performance validation. The intrinsic and MCTS tracks are separate experiments and must be analyzed separately.

## Reproducibility and output artifacts

The experiment scripts are designed to save run configurations and detailed outputs rather than only final averages. Depending on the package, outputs include:

- configuration and completion metadata;
- per-episode training records and individual greedy-evaluation outcomes;
- seed-level and pooled summaries;
- learning curves and evaluation plots;
- learned Q-tables and model snapshots;
- prediction/reliability audit records;
- script hashes and runtime-version metadata in the newer experiment suites;
- paired bootstrap intervals and/or paired tests where implemented.

Run names, output schemas, and the precise statistical unit can differ between experiment packages. Read each package's README and preserve the original raw archives. A smoke test establishes that code executes and writes artifacts; **it does not establish scientific validity or performance**.

For paired comparisons, the analysis uses maze–seed units rather than treating evaluation episodes from the same trained policy as independent experimental seeds. The reported uncertainty is conditional on the fixed layouts used in each experiment; it should not be generalized to all possible mazes without broader environment sampling.

## Limitations and interpretation safeguards

1. **Handcrafted environments:** the mazes are fixed layouts. The number of seeds does not substitute for testing a broad distribution of environments.
2. **Synthetic corruption:** model bias is injected into planning predictions only. Real environment transitions and real-transition Q-updates remain uncorrupted. This does not represent every natural model-error source.
3. **Reliability is heuristic:** prediction agreement / predictive skill is not a guarantee of calibrated uncertainty, and stochastic outcomes can make reliability scoring difficult.
4. **Results are conditional:** the method helps on the original deterministic benchmark but is not uniformly best under stochastic transitions.
5. **Compute fairness:** Dyna updates, prioritized sweeping, intrinsic-reward methods, and MCTS perform different amounts and kinds of computation. Do not interpret these results as a compute-matched leaderboard.
6. **Exploration baselines are adaptations:** RND-, ICM-, and NGU-style variants use a discrete maze state space and are not exact reproductions of the original high-dimensional algorithms.
7. **MCTS is inconclusive:** the tested hybrid did not learn reliably on target mazes. It is excluded from the main comparison; this is not evidence against MCTS in general.
8. **Unresolved evaluation anomaly:** the larger-grid Q-learning runs showed a gap between a late-training metric and greedy evaluation success. That gap should be investigated before drawing conclusions about Q-learning on those layouts.
9. **No biological claim:** the experiments are tabular computational analogies. They do not model hippocampal neural dynamics, replay sequences, or dopamine circuits.
10. **No convergence guarantee:** the project does not establish a general convergence theorem for reliability-weighted Dyna-Q.
